# Throwback Multitrack decisions

- Reference: Chris Cruise's TikTok at https://www.tiktok.com/@djchriscruise/video/7687979489258638606. The visible challenge reveals drums first, then bass, then more layers, while asking viewers to name the song as early as possible.
- The game keeps a cumulative, timed five-stage reveal: drums, bass, guitar/keys, other, then vocals. Players choose among three illustrated sleeves; a wrong pick is removed and drops the next stem. A correct guess scores 5 down to 1 points by stage.
- Daily mode chooses the same puzzle for every player on a UTC date, saves one result per browser, and provides spoiler-free share text. Practice mode plays the whole catalog.
- Visual direction after Kadi's feedback: the turntable is the start control, the countdown is a large console display, five channel strips show stem state, and the next strip can be tapped to drop it early. Explanatory prose is reduced to cues; the guessing and scoring rules stay intact.
- Second visual direction: an old-school wood and metal rack frames the game, with recessed mixer channels and three askew answer sleeves. The result becomes a tilted, colorful collectible card with original cover artwork, artist, title and year. The saved-result explanation was removed from the start screen.
- 2026-09-25, Kadi: ship both crates, publicly deployed but "only game nights with a password". Every file under /throwback-multitrack/, audio included, sits behind a Pages Functions password gate (12-hour unlock, noindex). The hub card stays a NEXT UP workshop card, so strangers aren't sent to a locked door.
- Public-safe crate: 12 Kevin MacLeod tracks (CC BY 4.0), which people know from a decade of YouTube videos. CC BY allows excerpting and splitting with credit, so each reveal card and the MUSIC CREDITS list carry the incompetech credit with "excerpted and split into instrument stems". The year hint is the track's incompetech upload year.
- Real hits (the TikTok's kind of song) go in a separate `hits` crate, split from files Kadi owns. Its audio stays out of git and plays only behind the gate. Publishing split stems of commercial songs openly would be infringement.
- Separation is Demucs htdemucs_6s on the CPU (Apple's MPS backend lacks ops it needs), run by `scripts/split.py` (replaces `prepare-song.mjs`). Songs have 3–5 layers, not a fixed 5: quiet stems fold into "The rest" rather than play as silent channels. Points run 5 → 1 across however many layers a song has.
